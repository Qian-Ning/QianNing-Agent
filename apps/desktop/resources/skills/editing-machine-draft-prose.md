A draft that reads as machine-written is not usually wrong. It is smooth, it is
grammatical, and every third sentence has the same shape. The work is breaking
that shape without wrecking what the sentence was for.

## The tells, with the fix

**The balanced antithesis.** *Not a bug, but a design decision.* Used twice a
page it stops being a thought and becomes a tic. Say the thing: *It was a design
decision.* If the contrast really matters, make the reader feel it in the facts
rather than in the syntax.

**The triad.** Three adjectives, three clauses, three examples, again and again.
Two is usually enough and four is usually better, because three is the shape a
model reaches for when it has nothing to count.

**Announcing instead of doing.** *It is worth noting that…* — then note it. *One
could argue…* — argue it or drop it. *In conclusion…* — the reader can see the
end.

**Hedge stacked on hedge.** *This may potentially suggest that it could…* Pick
the confidence you actually have and write at that level.

**Abstract nouns doing concrete work.** *The implementation of the solution* →
*the fix*. English buries verbs; dig them out.

**The closing sentence that restates the paragraph.** Cut it. If the paragraph
needed the summary, it was two paragraphs.

**Vocabulary from nowhere.** *delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, navigate,
robust, seamless, leverage.* Not banned — but each one is a flag, and the reader
has seen the flag before.

**Uniform sentence length.** Count words across ten sentences. If they cluster,
split one and join two others.

## How to actually revise

1. **Read it aloud, or at the pace of speech.** The ear catches what the eye
   forgives.
2. **Find the claim in each paragraph and put it in the first sentence.** Then
   delete the wind-up that preceded it.
3. **Convert one abstraction per paragraph into something a camera could
   record.** A number, a name, an object, an action.
4. **Check the verbs.** If most are forms of *to be* or *to have*, the paragraph
   has no motion. Give it real verbs.
5. **Vary the openings.** If three paragraphs start with a subject-verb clause,
   start one with a prepositional phrase, a question, or a fragment.
6. **Cut ten percent.** Not to be shorter — to find which sentences were
   carrying nothing.

## What not to do

- **Do not inject errors to seem human.** Typos are not voice.
- **Do not swap one stock phrase for another.** *delve into* → *dig into* is
  still the same move.
- **Do not flatten a deliberate register.** Technical writing, legal writing,
  and poetry have their own conventions; smoothness is not the goal there.
- **Do not rewrite beyond the brief.** Fix what was asked. Where you change a
  line, say what it was and why; leave the rest alone.
