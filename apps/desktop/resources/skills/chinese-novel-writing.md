Chinese prose written by a model reads like a press release wearing a costume.
The tells are specific and they are fixable, one at a time.

## The tells

**Uniform paragraph rhythm.** Every paragraph three sentences, every sentence
the same length. Real prose breathes unevenly: one line stands alone, the next
runs long. Read the page aloud; where you do not pause for breath, break it.

**Idiom as filler.** 不禁、仿佛、宛如、瞬间、顿时、心中一紧 — each one is a
half-second the reader stops seeing and starts recognizing. One per chapter is
character voice. Five per page is a machine.

**Emotion announced instead of performed.** 他非常生气 tells the reader a fact.
摔门, then 站在原地没动 does the work. Cut the adjective, keep the verb, and let
the body say it: hands, jaw, the exact sound a chair makes.

**Four-character parallelism everywhere.** 冷冷清清、凄凄惨惨、瑟瑟发抖. It is a
register for poetry and certain narrators, not a default texture. Use it when
the narrator's voice calls for it, and then use it deliberately.

**Dialogue that explains.** Characters stating what both already know, so the
reader can overhear it. Real speech is partial, interrupted, and answers a
different question than the one asked. Let a character refuse to answer.

**The narrator apologizing.** 也许、似乎、好像、不知为何 stacked until nothing is
asserted. Chinese narrative can be uncertain, but the uncertainty should be a
choice, not a hedge on every verb.

## Repair, in order

1. **Read it aloud.** Where your tongue stumbles or your breath runs out, the
   sentence is wrong. This catches more than any rule below.
2. **Replace abstraction with a noun the eye can see.** 环境很差 → 墙角的霉斑长
   到了插座上. One concrete detail beats three adjectives.
3. **Break the rhythm.** Take the longest sentence in the paragraph and cut it
   in half. Take the shortest and add one clause.
4. **Cut every 的 that is not needed.** 他的父亲的旧书 → 他父亲那本旧书.
5. **Demote the emotion to a consequence.** Not 她很难过, but 她把碗放回桌上，放
   了两次才放稳.
6. **Give each speaker one verbal habit** — a particle, a dropped subject, a
   sentence they always leave unfinished — and keep it consistent. That is what
   separates voices, not different words for "said".

## Punctuation and typography

- 全角标点 for Chinese text: ，。？！：；“”‘’《》 —— never the half-width forms.
- 破折号 use the paired ——, not a single — or a hyphen.
- 省略号 is ……, two groups, not ...
- Dialogue: keep the quotation marks consistent through the whole piece, and put
  the attribution before the speech when the speaker matters, after when the
  content matters.

## When the piece is translated or bilingual

Keep names, terms, and punctuation consistent with whichever convention the
document already uses. Do not silently switch a character's name between
transliterations, and do not translate an honorific that the surrounding text
keeps in the original.

## What not to do

- Do not replace one cliché with another; replacing 宛如 with 好似 fixes nothing.
- Do not strip a narrator's voice to fix pacing. The rhythm problem and the
  voice are different problems; solve the rhythm and keep the voice.
- Do not rewrite a passage the user did not ask you to touch. Quote the line you
  changed and why, and leave the rest intact.
